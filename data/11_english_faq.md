# Hydroponics FAQ for Beginners (English)

## What water should I use for hydroponics?
Tap water is usually fine for home growers. If your tap water is chlorinated, leave it in an open container for 24 hours so the chlorine can evaporate (this does not remove chloramine). Measure the EC of your source water first: if it is above 0.5 mS/cm, the water already contains a lot of minerals, and reverse osmosis (RO) or filtered water may give better results. Rainwater can be used if it is collected in a clean container, but it has no buffering capacity, so the pH may swing more.

## Can I use regular garden fertilizer instead of hydroponic nutrients?
It is not recommended. Common soil fertilizers such as 15-15-15 or 46-0-0 (urea) lack calcium, magnesium and most micronutrients, and urea-based nitrogen is designed to be converted by soil microbes, which are mostly absent in hydroponic systems. Plants grown with soil fertilizer in water often show yellow leaves and tipburn. Use a complete two-part hydroponic nutrient (A and B) instead.

## How much oxygen do roots need?
Roots need dissolved oxygen (DO) to breathe. A good target is above 6 mg/L. In DWC systems, an air pump with an air stone is required. In NFT systems, the thin film of water and the air gap in the channel provide oxygen. Warm water holds less oxygen: at 20°C water can hold about 9 mg/L, but at 30°C only about 7.5 mg/L.

## Healthy roots versus unhealthy roots
Healthy hydroponic roots are white or creamy, firm, and have many fine root hairs. Brown, slimy, smelly roots usually mean root rot caused by Pythium. Note that roots may be stained light brown by some nutrients or by coco coir, so always check the texture and smell, not just the colour.

## Do I need to clean the system between crops?
Yes. After each harvest, empty the reservoir, scrub channels and tanks to remove algae and root residue, and sanitize with a dilute solution of hydrogen peroxide or a mild bleach solution (about 1 part household bleach to 100 parts water). Rinse thoroughly with clean water before starting the next crop, because residual bleach will damage young roots.

## Can I reuse grow sponges or net pots?
Net pots can be washed, sanitized, and reused many times. Sponges and rockwool cubes should not be reused, because old roots stay inside them and they can carry disease to the next crop. Clay pebbles (LECA) can be reused after washing and sanitizing.

## Is hydroponic produce organic?
Under most organic certification standards, including the USDA in the United States, hydroponic produce can be certified organic only if the nutrients come from approved organic sources. Most hydroponic farms use mineral (synthetic) nutrients, so their vegetables are classified as "pesticide-free" or "safe vegetables" rather than "organic". Hydroponic vegetables are safe to eat when growers follow good practices and reduce nitrate before harvest.

## How long does a power outage take to harm my plants?
It depends on the system. In NFT, roots can dry out in 2-4 hours on a hot day. In DFT and DWC, plants can survive much longer because roots remain in water, although oxygen will slowly decrease. Kratky systems use no electricity at all. If you live in an area with frequent outages, consider a battery backup for the pump or choose a deep-water system.

## Can children help with a home hydroponic garden?
Yes. Seeding sponges, observing germination, and harvesting are great learning activities. Keep concentrated A-B nutrients and pH Up / pH Down solutions out of reach of children and pets, because they are corrosive chemicals.
